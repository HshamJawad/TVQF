About TVQF Iraq

The TVQF Iraq is designed to establish a coherent national framework for qualifications across all levels—from basic vocational training through to advanced doctoral awards. Its aim is to enhance transparency, comparability and recognition of learning outcomes for learners, employers and institutions alike.

The framework provides a systematic approach to organizing qualifications by level and learning outcomes, making it easier for individuals to progress in their education and career paths, and for employers to understand the competencies associated with different qualifications.

Background & Development

Initiated in 2017 and approved by Council of Ministers Decision 99 (2021), TVQF Iraq reflects a commitment to reforming the education-training system and strengthening alignment with the evolving labour market and international standards.

The development of TVQF Iraq has been a collaborative effort involving stakeholders from across the education and training sector, supported by international partners including UNESCO and the European Union. The framework represents years of consultation, research, and policy development to create a system that meets Iraq's specific needs while aligning with global best practices.

Governance

The framework is overseen by a National Coordination Committee comprising representatives from the Ministries of Education, Higher Education & Research, and Labour & Social Affairs. Their mandate includes policy oversight, stakeholder coordination and guidance for institutional implementation.

The governance structure ensures that TVQF Iraq remains responsive to the needs of learners, employers, and the broader economy. Regular reviews and stakeholder consultations help maintain the framework's relevance and effectiveness.

What is RPL?

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) allows learners to obtain credit for learning acquired outside formal education—through work experience, informal training or other means. RPL supports inclusive access to qualifications, improved mobility and full recognition of competencies.

Benefits of RPL

  • Recognition of skills and knowledge gained through work experience
  • Shortened pathway to formal qualifications
  • Cost and time savings for learners
  • Enhanced employment opportunities
  • Increased access to further education and training
  • Validation of informal and non-formal learning

What Can Be Recognized?

RPL can be granted for learning acquired through:

  • Workplace experience and on-the-job training
  • Informal training and workshops
  • Self-directed learning and study
  • Volunteer work and community activities
  • Life experience relevant to the qualification

How to Apply for RPL

Follow this step-by-step guide to apply for Recognition of Prior Learning.

Step 1: Check Eligibility

Ensure you have relevant work experience or informal learning that aligns with the qualification you're seeking. Most RPL applications require a minimum of 2-3 years of relevant experience.

Step 2: Gather Documentation

Collect evidence of your learning and experience, including:

  • Employment certificates and job descriptions
  • Training certificates from informal courses
  • Work samples and portfolios
  • Reference letters from employers or supervisors
  • Any other relevant documentation

Step 3: Complete the Application

Fill out the RPL Application Form completely and attach all supporting documents.

📂 Go to TVQF Forms & Templates

Step 4: Submit to RPL Centre

Submit your completed application to your nearest RPL Centre. A list of centres is available on the RPL Centres page.

Step 5: Assessment

Your application will be reviewed by qualified assessors who will evaluate your evidence against the learning outcomes of the qualification. Assessment methods may include:

  • Portfolio review
  • Interview or oral questioning
  • Practical demonstration
  • Written test (if necessary)

Step 6: Receive Results

You will be notified of the assessment outcome, which may be:

  • Full recognition - credit granted for the entire qualification or units
  • Partial recognition - credit granted for some units, with gap training required
  • Not recognized - insufficient evidence, with recommendations for further learning

National Pilot Plan Launch

October 15, 2025

The TVQF Iraq National Pilot Plan (2025-2028) has been officially launched at a ceremony in Baghdad attended by ministers, international partners, and representatives from pilot institutions. The plan outlines a phased approach to implementing the framework across all governorates, starting with 20 pilot institutions in key sectors including engineering, IT, healthcare, and construction.

The Minister of Education emphasized that TVQF Iraq represents a transformative step toward modernizing Iraq's education system and ensuring qualifications are recognized both nationally and internationally.

Institutional Accreditation Applications Now Open

September 28, 2025

Training providers across Iraq can now submit applications for institutional accreditation through the official TVQF portal. The accreditation process ensures that institutions meet quality standards and are authorized to issue TVQF-aligned qualifications.

Three information sessions will be held in Baghdad, Basra, and Erbil during October to help institutions prepare their applications. Registration is open on the TVQF website.

RPL Pilot Centres Established

August 12, 2025

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) pilot centres have been established in Baghdad, Basra, Erbil, and Mosul. These centres will assess applications from workers and learners seeking credit for skills and knowledge gained through work experience and informal learning.

Over 50 assessors have been trained to conduct RPL evaluations across multiple sectors. The centres are expected to process their first applications by November 2025.

Sectoral Learning Outcome Matrices in Development

Technical working groups are developing detailed Sectoral Learning Outcome Matrices for Engineering, Information Technology, and Health sectors. These matrices will provide clear competency frameworks to guide program design and ensure consistent standards across institutions.

Draft matrices are expected to be released for stakeholder consultation in December 2025.

Qualification Types

The framework describes how certificates, diplomas and degrees align with national levels. Understanding these qualification types helps learners and employers navigate the education system effectively.

Vocational Training Certificates (Levels 1-3)

Short-cycle programs focused on practical skills for immediate employment in specific trades and occupations.

Secondary Vocational Diplomas (Level 4)

Comprehensive programs combining theoretical knowledge with practical skills, typically completed after secondary education.

Technical Diplomas and First Degrees (Levels 5-6)

Higher education qualifications that provide specialized knowledge and advanced competencies in professional fields.

Postgraduate Qualifications (Levels 7-10)

Advanced academic and professional qualifications for research, specialized practice, and leadership roles.

Credit & Learning Hours

TVQF Iraq uses a provisional credit system (e.g., 25 learning hours = 1 credit). This supports transparent recognition of program volume, credit transfer and mobility between learning pathways.

The credit system facilitates:

  • Clear understanding of the workload required for each qualification
  • Recognition of prior learning and credit transfer between institutions
  • Flexibility in learning pathways and program design
  • Alignment with international credit systems for global mobility

Each credit represents a defined amount of learning time, including contact hours, independent study, and assessment preparation. This transparent approach helps learners plan their educational journey and institutions design effective programs.

Institutional Accreditation

Training providers must gain accreditation to issue TVQF-aligned qualifications. The process involves submitting evidence of governance, curriculum, assessment, staffing and continuous improvement to the Accreditation Committee.

Accreditation Requirements

  • Clear organizational governance and management structures
  • Qualified and experienced teaching staff
  • Appropriate facilities and learning resources
  • Quality assurance and continuous improvement systems
  • Curriculum aligned with TVQF learning outcomes
  • Valid and reliable assessment practices
  • Student support services and safe learning environment

Qualification Approval

Institutions can apply for official level recognition for each program through the Qualification Leveling Application Form. The Sectoral Committee reviews and recommends level placement.

Qualification approval requests go through an internal and external evaluation process overseen by the TVQF Board/Authority and its specialized committees — including the Quality Control Committee — which review the submitted forms and documentation and issue decisions on accreditation and level placement.

Application Process

  1. Complete the Qualification Leveling Application Form
  2. Provide detailed program documentation including learning outcomes, curriculum, and assessment methods
  3. Submit to the relevant Sectoral Committee for review
  4. Participate in field visits or interviews as required
  5. Receive decision and feedback from the committee
  6. Implement any required modifications
  7. Obtain final approval and level placement

Monitoring & Evaluation

Accredited institutions will be subject to monitoring cycles and periodic reviews to ensure continuous alignment with TVQF standards. Tools and feedback mechanisms support improvement.

Monitoring Activities

  • Regular field visits by quality assurance teams
  • Annual reporting on program delivery and outcomes
  • Student feedback and satisfaction surveys
  • Employer feedback on graduate competencies
  • Review of assessment results and graduate destinations
  • Periodic re-accreditation reviews

The monitoring process is designed to be supportive and developmental, helping institutions identify areas for improvement while recognizing good practices.
